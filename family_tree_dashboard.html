🔲 Grid Bot

This is the live crypto engine on this account - the only crypto system currently placing real Coinbase orders. Each branch buys a real slice when its coin closes one grid step below that branch's own reference price, then sells the oldest slice that is genuinely net-profitable at the current price - never one that would realize a loss just because an unrelated, lower slice moved the reference. Real fees both ways. Its own capital and position tracking; it never shares state with the family tree below.

The step is chosen per coin from measured grid performance, not from how the coin trends - a coin can be a poor hold and an excellent grid. Over 90 days of real candles a wider step beat a tighter one on six of seven coins ($128.30 vs $23.24 at 1.70%), and it also produced more round trips, not fewer: selling early resets the reference upward and costs the next entry. No step may ever sit below the fee floor shown below.

Idle cash no longer moves on its own. Automatic rotation was removed on 2026-09-25 after it retired four earning branches to cash on directional ROI - DOGE went out at -23.8% directional while measuring +$22.81 on the grid, and 64% of the account sat idle until someone noticed. Cash now moves only when you move it. Each branch still carries a drawdown circuit breaker at 25% (pauses NEW buys only; open slices keep selling), and that breaker is honest about itself: on 90 days of these coins it never fired at any setting from 10% to 25%, so treat it as untested insurance rather than proven protection.

📟 The tape · other people’s trades, not yours
Every trade printing as it happens on the coins you hold - the same display Kalshi puts beside its price chart, where the floating +$19 / +$24 are individual trades executing. It is called a trade tape, or time & sales. It exists because a price alone tells you where the market is, while the tape tells you whether anything is actually happening at that price - a chart sitting still and a chart sitting still with nothing trading are the same picture and completely different situations.

These are other people's trades, not yours. Coinbase publishes every fill on the venue, so this is the whole market's flow on your holdings. This account is not currently placing orders, so a tape of your own fills would be an empty box.

🧪 Did it earn that, or did the coin go up?
The resting-rung study measured three coins over the same 21 days. BONK rose 19.27% and its best rung made money. ONDO rose 16.52% and did the same. BTC fell 2.61% and every rung lost - every target, every horizon. Its widest setting filled 0% of the time and returned exactly the window return, marking a leg that never opened.

Two rose and it won; one fell and it lost everywhere. That is beta - the return of simply holding - wearing a strategy's name.

The useful part is the asymmetry. A negative result in a rising market is robust: a long-only rung that loses 1.03% while its coin rose 19.27% really loses, and no regime excuse exists. A positive result in a rising market proves nothing yet. So tight-and-fast can be ruled OUT today, while wide-and-slow cannot be ruled IN until something falls.

None of this judges the grid. The grid has a real closed book - 83 round trips, 75.9% won, 3.70 profit factor at a 21-hour average hold.

🩹 The losses — how big, and why
A grid that never sells at a loss already exists. It is called holding. The sell path already refuses to force a losing sale, so the only thing that books a loss here is the stop - which means "no losses at all" is one setting away, and it is the wrong setting.

Switch the stop off and a slice that falls 40% is simply held forever, waiting for a +2.5% that now has to come from a much lower price. The loss does not disappear - it stops being counted and starts being inventory. That is exactly how a 0.9% grid step took this fleet from +65.4% to −71.1%: nothing was ever realised as a loss and everything was stuck.

So the goal that can actually be reached is a small average loss against a large average win, with the stop distance chosen from the recorded excursions rather than from an opinion. Every slice records how far it ever went against its entry, which is what makes "would a 5% stop have beaten the 8% one?" answerable on the real closed book instead of by a backtest that also changes which trades happened.

👑 The league — every coin chasing the crown
The crown cannot be units. PEPE holds 20,232,619 of them and is worth $87.61 - the largest unit count in the account and the smallest position on the page - because unit count is set by a token's supply and nothing else. Chasing it would mean buying billions of the cheapest thing available.

It cannot be dollars earned either: a coin with $200 behind it will always out-earn one with $20, which measures the allocation, not the coin.

So the league ranks on return per dollar risked, per round trip, net of fees. An $87 position and a $2,250 position produce directly comparable numbers - which is the point. The small coin can genuinely take the crown off the big one, and then every other coin has something real to chase.

The crown is PROVISIONAL while the leader's own price rose, and CONFIRMED only once it earned through a flat or falling market. Without that the league crowns whatever pumped and the blueprint copies luck to every coin in the account. The blueprint hands over the settings, never the market - that is the part any coin can adopt, including a weak one.

🌱 Put the coin you already own to work
$10,526 of this account is coin, and $10,794 of it belongs to no branch. It cannot be put to work today for one structural reason: a grid branch is funded with cash and buys its own rungs, and nothing in the engine can hand a branch units you already hold.

Adoption closes that with bookkeeping, not trading. A branch opens on a coin you already own, and the existing units are registered as open slices at that day's price. Nothing is bought. Nothing is sold. The branch starts full instead of starting in cash, and from then on it sells into strength and rebuys lower.

What it costs, plainly: adopted coin stops being held and starts being traded. Over the prior 90 days holding beat the grid by 16.13 points, because grids underperform a rally. That is why this is capped at a test slice - a few hundred dollars across at most three coins, run alongside the two-week verdict, so that verdict gets real evidence instead of a guess.

🔬 Why the profit is that size
"$19.61 realized" answers one question and hides four. It cannot tell you whether the figure is small because the edge is thin, because the capital is tiny, because the money sits idle, or because the wins are being given back - and those have four different fixes, only one of which is a strategy change.

So this measures how hard each dollar works, not just how much was made, and then names the one binding cause instead of listing symptoms. Naming the wrong one sends the next month of work in the wrong direction.

Capital velocity is here because it is the headline of the plan this was built from - but it is shown in plain grey, never green, and never without the edge beside it. At a 0.9% grid step this fleet cycled five times the capital it does at 3.75% and went from +65.4% to -71.1%. Velocity multiplies the edge; it does not replace it, and a system optimising velocity while its edge is negative is optimising the speed at which it loses.
