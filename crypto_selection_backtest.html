Can we make money off the stops themselves? Right after the exit-reason breakdown showed that a real losing window's damage was mostly OLD exit types and structural forced exits rather than genuine price-based stops, the direct question came back: does price tend to recover after a real STOP HIT, and would buying back in right at the stop-exit price have actually made money? This replays that against the FULL real historical STOP HIT ledger (every coin, every real hard-stop exit ever recorded - not just one 20-trade window): the real forward price move in the hours after each stop, whether it ever recovered back to the stop's own exit price, and a simple hypothetical "buy back at the stop price, sell at a modest target or a second hard stop" trade. Shadow mode only - purely diagnostic, never wired to any real trade. No fees are modeled on the hypothetical trades and real cash availability isn't checked, so treat this as a real, honest first look, not a final answer.
What about the OTHER real losses - the forced exits? The exit-reason breakdown showed most of a real losing window's damage actually came from BRANCH BREACH / EQUITY FLOOR BREACH - a branch's own real floor/drawdown-breach safety net force-selling it, not a genuine price-based stop. Unlike the OLD exit types from before QUICK_PROFIT was removed (which can never happen again), these two are real and still live today - so the same reversal question applies: does price tend to recover after one of these forced exits, and would buying back in right at that exit price have made money? Same real methodology as the Stop-Hit test above (real, fee-adjusted P&L on the hypothetical reversal trade), same one remaining honest limitation (real cash availability not checked) - only the source exit type differs. Shadow mode only.

The Grid Bot's fee assumption also reuses this file's existing real round-trip rate rather than the lower "maker-only" rate the pasted proposal assumed - worth confirming Coinbase's actual current fee schedule before trusting that difference either way. 🎯 Narrow-range breakout continuation - does it really hold? A real trading concept the account owner described directly: "the best opportunity come from a narrow state... if you open above a narrow state... 87% chance there are more upside to come... if you open below a narrow state... 87% chance to follow through to the downside." That 87% is their own stated number, not yet checked against this system's real data. This replays every real coin's history looking for genuinely NARROW price ranges (tight relative to that SAME coin's own recent range history, not one fixed threshold - a coin's normal volatility varies too much for a flat number to mean the same thing everywhere), then checks the real FIRST candle that breaks outside each narrow range and whether price genuinely continued in that direction 24 real hours later. Reports the real hit rate, split by up vs. down breakouts, against an honest 50% coin-flip baseline. Shadow mode only - diagnostic, never wired to any real trade or entry filter.
