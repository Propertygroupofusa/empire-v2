## 5. The venue record against ours  (CORRECTED 2026-09-26, order counts)

The first version of this section said 74% of executions never reached the
ledger. That was wrong twice over: it counted event contracts our bots have
never traded, and it counted FILLS rather than ORDERS, so partial filling was
read as extra trading. Both are fixed below. The exchange numbers did not
change; the arithmetic on top of them did.

ALL OF THIS IS COINBASE. Every fill here came back from Coinbase's own
/orders/historical/fills on this one account. The rows whose product ids read
KXBTC15M-...-KALSHI are Coinbase EVENT CONTRACTS - bought on Coinbase, paid
for with Coinbase commission; the -KALSHI tag names the exchange the contract
settles on, not a second account somewhere else. They are separated out below
only because they are a different PRODUCT CLASS, with a different fee schedule
and no liquidity_indicator, and because no code in this repository can
construct such a product id - every Coinbase order path here is built from a
-USD spot pair. A grid ledger row therefore cannot correspond to one of those
fills, so counting them made the gap look two thirds larger than it is.

CONFIRMED BY THE ACCOUNT OWNER: this was manual trading on 2026-09-06, done
by hand, not by anything in this repository. That settles the question the
figure raised. What it does NOT settle is the accounting: this spending must
be kept out of every system metric, because it is not a cost of running the
strategy and including it makes the strategy look four times more expensive
than it is.

What the fills feed CANNOT say: a contract that was bought and never
sold expired. Expiry settles as a balance credit, not a fill, so the feed
shows those as "still held". $3,496.95 of net outflow sits in that category
and its outcome is not knowable from this endpoint. The commission is
knowable and is not recoverable either way.

### The ledger understates the loss

    entry-leg commission never booked         $   -94.97

The +$332.88 is not a gain. Those 11 rows were charged for coin they did
not sell, so the loss they record never happened at that size - but the
coin behind the over-charge was cleared from its position and is still
unaccounted for. The books get more honest; the account does not get
richer.

RATE NOTE. An earlier version of this section put the entry-leg figure at
$176.41. That applied today's 1.5% constant to rows booked under an 0.8%
schedule. Solved row by row, the rows state their own rate - median 0.00800
across 148 of 156 - and the real figure is $94.97. Correcting history at a
later fee schedule invents charges nobody paid.

At an average notional of $107.48 per close, the fee toll is $1.28 a round
trip and the recorded result is -$1.96. The strategy is not losing badly on
direction - it is losing roughly its own fee. That is the whole problem,
and it is the same conclusion the step study reached from the other side.

## 6. Findings, worst first  (CORRECTED)

    1. RESOLVED. The event-contract spending was the account owner trading by
       hand on 2026-09-06. Not a rogue process, not a bug, not a security
       problem - a one-off, confirmed by the owner. It is separated from every
       system metric from here on, because mixing it in was making the
       automated strategy look four times more expensive than it is.
    2. The tree's ledger charged ONE fee leg, not two. Its rows omit the
       entry commission - $94.97 across the 156 that are otherwise
       consistent, priced at the 0.8% schedule those rows were actually
       booked under. Every row reads better than the trade was. The formula
       is fixed and a correction for the existing rows is built and
       previewed; applying it needs the write token.
    3. -2.54% of the account is tracked. $11,643.04 has no stop, no
       monitoring and no owner in the system. It is also the part that has been
       growing.
    4. $381.47 of branch allocation is unbacked - a claim, not money.
    5. Neither venue can trade: Coinbase cash is under its fee reserve, Alpaca
       buying power is under its floor with unsettled proceeds.
    6. Four assets cannot be priced, so the real total is higher than stated by
       an unknown amount.
    7. A 32-close gap (11.4%) between the exchange and our ledgers. Real, worth
       closing, but small enough that it does not invalidate the modelling.
    8. Ten dust positions are below any tradeable size and cannot be recovered.
